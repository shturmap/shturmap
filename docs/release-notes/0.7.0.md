**Shturmap 0.7.0 "NAME"**

Shturmap is a second-monitor map for Escape from Tarkov: your active quests' objectives, the extracts and your
position on the map of the raid you're in, in one window beside the game.

**New since 0.6.2.**

- **The stash desk: what your stash and gear are worth.** Between raids, take a screenshot of your stash or of the
  GEAR tab (your loadout beside the stash) with the game's own screenshot key: Shturmap reads its item grids on your
  PC against every item tarkov.dev lists, and the desk opens in the map's place, the screenshot mirrored cell for
  cell. Each cell carries what the item is worth, the better of the flea's net after the fee and the best trader's
  price, with a tag for the flea or a trader's sign before it; a "~" where the figure is an estimate from condition, a
  "+" where a container's contents or a gun's mods weren't seen, an arrow where its price moved 10 % or more in 48
  hours, and a notch in its corner where a quest needs it, now, next or later (or a hideout module). Over the page,
  what is on you against what is in the stash, and how many items to keep: **ON YOU · STASH · KEEP**. A new
  screenshot opens the parts it changed and folds the rest into one line, so after a raid what came back stands first.
  A container whose window you opened in the game gets its own grid, tied to its cell. **PLAN | DESK** at the top left
  switches between the planner and the desk; a raid loading takes the window back by itself, and the desk keeps its
  page for next time. Pointing at an item shows its item card for now; the desk's own card, with the price's history,
  and a click on a cell that copies a flea price to paste in the game come next. What is kept: the page, as item ids
  with where each lay, their counts, condition and found-in-raid marks and the screenshot's time (`desk.json` in the
  data folder), no pixels, until a newer screenshot changes it; a screenshot taken in a raid is never read for it.
  The prices are tarkov.dev's item file, asked for again when a screenshot is read for the desk and the saved copy is
  over an hour old: at most once an hour, about 1.4 MB, the same file for everyone. The setting **Read your stash, gear
  and tasks from menu screenshots, and loot in a raid** covers it. Measured on 2560×1440 screenshots of the English
  game; other sizes and languages aren't checked yet.
- **Your dailies and weeklies, on their maps.** The game's daily and weekly operational tasks are in neither its logs
  nor tarkov.dev's data, so take a screenshot of the Tasks screen on its OPERATIONAL tab, with the game's own
  screenshot key: Shturmap reads the table on your PC (each task's name, location, time left and progress, and for the
  row you opened, its objectives; never the rewards). A cue lists what it read ("3 DAILIES AND WEEKLIES"), soonest to
  end first. Then each task stands where its map's work is: a framed symbol on the map's NEXT RAID row, and an
  **OPERATIONAL** section in the open map's card and in the raid card, under PICKED, with the time left; a task for
  any location shows in every map's card. In a raid the card says **ENDS IN 25 MIN** in red when a task ends before the
  raid does, and a Scav's raid lists the items a task wants handed over under FIND IN RAID. Screenshots taken within a
  minute of each other are one reading, so a table and a row opened after it make one cue. A task leaves the list as it
  runs out, and the card says how many ended. The order of the maps doesn't change: the tasks don't count as quest
  work. The setting **Read your stash, gear and tasks from menu screenshots, and loot in a raid** covers it. The game
  must run in English for now; the table was read on 2560×1440 screenshots, and other sizes aren't checked yet.
- **Your loot, at a glance, in a raid.** Take a screenshot in a raid with your inventory open beside a body or a
  container you loot: Shturmap reads its item grids on your PC, after the extract list's corner, and a plate at the top
  of the map says for about six seconds what the loot is worth, laid out as the game shows it (a body's slots, rig,
  pockets and backpack, or the container's grid), each item with its price as on the desk; a part not searched yet says
  UNSEARCHED and its total takes a "+". Your own gear isn't drawn; its total stands under the loot's. Nothing of it is
  kept, and it goes when the raid ends. The setting **Read your stash, gear and tasks from menu screenshots, and loot
  in a raid** covers it; the game must run in English for now, and it was read on 2560×1440 screenshots of one raid.
- **Text as large as you need.** Windows' text size (Settings › Accessibility › Text size) reached every word of
  Shturmap's panels but not the map, which stayed at 100 %: it follows now, labels and symbols alike. **Text size** in
  settings adds a step of Shturmap's own on top of Windows': **NORMAL**, **LARGER** (×1.25) or **LARGEST** (×1.5), up to
  2.25 times in all, and changes the window as you pick it. The smallest window grows with the text, so the raid card's
  EXIT stays in view, and what was cut off at 125 and 150 % now wraps, grows or ends in "…", never in smaller text. At
  200 % it works, but cramped.
- **A required weapon, assembled.** A quest that needs a gun showed tarkov.dev's icon of its receiver alone, barely a
  weapon. Its cells now show the gun as the traders sell it (its default preset's icon): BRING, the kit cue, the quest
  card and the item card. Knives, grenades and under-barrel launchers have no such picture and keep their own.
- **Two symbols on one spot stay put.** tarkov.dev gives a few extracts the same id, Woods' two Outskirts (a PMC's and
  a Scav's, less than a metre apart) among them, and each pan or zoom pushed the pair further apart along parallel
  lines, until one stood off the map in a corner of the view. Each symbol now keeps its own place, and none stands
  further than 120 pixels (at 100 % display scale) from where it belongs.
- **The tour** names your gear and the tasks among what a menu screenshot is read for (chapter 1), and the desk
  beside the planner (chapter 2).

**This is an early release.** Expect rough edges. The story chapters (Falling Skies, Batya, …) aren't shown yet:
tarkov.dev doesn't publish them. If something is wrong or missing, tell us from the app: the **feedback** button at
the top right. No account needed. With a GitHub account you can also open an issue in this repository; issues are
public, so leave the game's logs, screenshots and ids out of them.

**Built where you can check it.** Every release is built and published by the repository's own GitHub Actions
workflow from the tagged commit, with a signed build-provenance attestation. To check that a download
is that build: `gh attestation verify Shturmap-Setup.exe -R shturmap/shturmap`, or compare its SHA-256
(`Get-FileHash Shturmap-Setup.exe`) with the `.sha256` file beside it. The README says what the tests check on every
push.

**What it reads, and what it never does.** Shturmap reads the game's own log files (quests started, completed and
failed, the items a quest hands you; the map you load into), the file names of your screenshots, which the game fills
with your position (Battlestate's support article on [reporting a bug](https://www.escapefromtarkov.com/support/knowledge/437)
describes these coordinates; a source, not an endorsement), the extract list in a raid screenshot's top right
corner, and in a screenshot you take in the menus, the item cells, for the items your quests need and, of your stash
and gear, against every item for the stash desk, and the Tasks screen's OPERATIONAL table, for your dailies and
weeklies (settings turn each off). It never reads the game's memory,
never sends input to the game, never captures the screen and never changes game files; its own code never opens the
game's process (the installer it comes with looks through running programs to close Shturmap when it installs or
updates: the README says exactly what that means). Outside its own folders it deletes one thing, and only with
**Delete screenshots after reading** ticked: a screenshot you just took, once read. It shows your position to you
only: it never shares it with anyone and never takes a screenshot for you. Data and map artwork come from tarkov.dev.

**Use at your own risk.** Shturmap is an unofficial fan project, not made or endorsed by Battlestate Games.
Battlestate's License Agreement (section 4.3.4) lets it decide which outside software it permits, so there is no
guarantee against sanctions. Some players see a map with your own position as an unfair advantage in PvP; Shturmap
is at its most useful for learning maps and in PvE.

**Install.** Download `Shturmap-Setup.exe` below and run it (64-bit Windows 10 2004 or later, or Windows 11). It
installs for your Windows user only, with no admin rights, and adds a Start-menu and a desktop shortcut. The installer
isn't signed, so Windows warns about it: click **More info → Run anyway**. Uninstall it under Windows Settings → Apps,
or settings (the gear) → **Uninstall Shturmap…**; your data in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Shturmap` stays unless you tick **Also
delete my Shturmap data** there.

**Updates.** Shturmap keeps itself up to date: it asks GitHub for a newer version at start and every 6 hours,
downloads it in the background and applies it the next time you start it, never during a raid. Settings (the gear) →
**Updates** switches between Automatic, Tell me only and Off.

**What is sent.** Nothing about you or your game, unless you send a report or allow crash reports. Its other
traffic downloads public data and map artwork, and asks GitHub for a newer version (settings → **Updates** turns that
off). **Privacy** in settings says exactly what a report holds and who receives it.
